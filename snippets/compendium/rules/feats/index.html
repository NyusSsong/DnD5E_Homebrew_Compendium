    Feats

    Actor

    Skilled at mimicry and dramatics, you gain the following benefits:

    • Impersonation. While you’re disguised as a real or fictional person, you have Advantage on Charisma (Deception or Performance) checks to convince others that you are that person.
    • Mimicry. You can mimic the sounds of other creatures, including speech. A creature that hears the mimicry must succeed on a Wisdom (Insight) check to determine the effect is faked (DC 8 + your Proficiency Bonus + your Charisma modifier).

    Acrobatic

    You are incredibly limber and agile, with a natural gift for gymnastics. You gain the following benefits:

    • Acrobatics Training. You gain proficiency in the Acrobatics skill.
    • Kip Up. When you are prone, standing up only uses 5 feet of your movement.
    • Acrobatic Movement. On your turn, your movement can include walking vertically or horizontally across a wall or similar structure. Every foot of movement across a wall costs you 1 extra foot.
    • Acrobatic Speed. When you take the Dash action, you may leap over objects 5-feet high or shorter without making an ability check.

    Adaptable

    Your adaptability is second to none. You gain three benefits from the following list. You can select a benefit more than once:

    • Skill Training. You gain proficiency in a skill of your choice.
    • Tool Training. You gain proficiency in a tool of your choice.
    • Language Training. You learn a new language of your chcoice.
    • Expertise Training. Choose one skill or tool in which you have proficiency. You gain expertise with that skill or tool. The skill or tool you choose must be one that isn’t already benefitting from a feature, such as Expertise, that doubles your Proficiency Bonus.

    Alert

    Always on the lookout for danger, you gain the following benefits:

    • Fast Reaction. You gain a bonus to your initiative rolls equal to your Proficiency Bonus.
    • Unsurprisable. You can’t be surprised while you are conscious. Additionally, other creatures don’t again advantage on attack rolls against you as a result of being unseen by you.
    • Initiative Swap. Immediately after you roll initiative, you can swap your initiative with the initiative of one willing ally in the same encounter. You can’t make the swap if either you or your ally are incapacitated.

    Ambidextrous

    When you fight with two weapons, your martial skill enables you to perform deadly maneuvers, relentlessly assaulting your opponents. You gain the following benefits:

    • Flurry of Blades. When you are wielding a Light melee weapon in each hand and take the Attack action, you can use a bonus action to attack twice with your second weapon instead of once.
    • Mirrored Impact. When you are wielding a Light melee weapon in each hand and have advantage on your attack rolls against a creature, if you hit and the lower of the two d20s would have also hit, you can immediately use your reaction to deal additional damage equal to your second weapon’s damage die. You do not add your ability modifier to this damage unless you have the Two-Weapon Fighting Style.

    Animal Affinity

    You have a way with animals. You gain the following benefits:

    • Animal Handling Training. You gain proficiency in the Animal Handling skill.
    • Animal Sense. You gain advantage on all Wisdom (Insight) checks when attempting to read a beast’s emotional state and its disposition.
    • Charm Animal. If you or your allies have not attacked a beast and it is not immediately hostile, you may attempt to charm it. Make a Wisdom (Animal Handling) check against a DC equal to 8 + the CR of the creature. If you succeed, the beast is charmed by you for 1 minute or until it takes any damage. If you fail, the beast is immune to this ability until you finish a long rest.

    Archivist

    You have learned to organize your notes and data in very ingenious ways, granting you the following benefits:

    • Note Gathering. Whenever you make an Intelligence (Arcana, Culture, Engineering, History, Nature, or Religion) check, you have advantage on this check if you spend at least 1 minute reading your notes and gathering your memories. You can use this benefit only once on the same subject, and only if you haven’t already used the benefit below in this subject.
    • Archivist Knowledge. Whenever you make an Intelligence (Arcana, Culture, Engineering, History, Nature, or Religion) check during a short rest, you can forsake your roll to instead resolve your check as if you had rolled a 15.

    Armor Mastery

    You have spent countless hours conditioning your body to the needs of wearing armor. You must be proficient in the type of armor you select for you to gain the associated benefits:

    • Light Armor Mastery. Your Armor Class increases by +1.
    • Medium Armor Mastery. The maximum Dexterity bonus allowed for your armor becomes +3 instead of +2. Additionally, you no longer have disadvantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks.
    • Heavy Armor Mastery. While you are wearing heavy armor, bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage that you take from attacks is reduced by an amount equal to your Proficiency Bonus.
    • Repeatable. You can take this feat more than once, but you must choose a different type of armor each time.

    Arms and Armor Training

    You have learned the proper use of armor and weapons. You gain the following benefit:

    • First Lesson in Arms and Armor Training. You gain proficiency in light and medium armor, simple and martial weapons, and shields.
    • Second Lesson in Arms and Armor Training. If you are already proficient in light armor and medium armor, or would later become proficient in them by other means, you instead gain proficiency in heavy armor.

    Athlete

    You have undergone extensive physical training to gain the following benefits:

    • Athletics Training. You gain proficiency in the Athletics skill.
    • Hop Up. When you are prone, standing up uses only 5 feet of your movement.
    • Climb Speed. You gain a climb speed equal to your speed.
    • Athletic Jump. You can make a running long jump or a running high jump after moving only 5 feet on foot, rather than 10 feet. Additionally, you have advantage on any ability check you make to jump higher than you normally can.

    Barfly

    As someone used to the rowdiness of nocturnal life and drinking establishments, you have developed the following benefits:

    • Seasoned Reveler. When you are poisoned, you do not have disadvantage on attack rolls, and you have advantage on saving throws against being frightened.
    • High-Stakes Hustler. You have advantage on ability checks associated with gambling games.
    • Smoke-Cut Eyes. Mist and fog, magical or not, hamper your vision less than normal. You suffer no negative effects from a lightly obscured area, and a heavily obscured area only gives you disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on sight.
    • Creature of the Night. You are a veritable night owl, with the vision to match. You gain darkvision out to a range of 30 feet. If you already have darkvision, its range increases by 30 feet.
    • Keen Instincts. You have advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks contested by a Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) or Dexterity (Stealth) check.

    Blind-Fighting

    Your training in fighting without the use of your eyes has heightened your other senses to a nearly magical degree. You gain the following benefits:

    • Echo Perception. You gain blindsight out to a 10-foot radius.
    • Unseen Reprisal. When an invisible or hidden creature that you are aware of makes a melee attack against you, you may make an opportunity attack against that creature.

    Bounty Hunter

    When there’s a price on someone’s head, you make it your business to collect. You gain the following benefits:

    • Photographic Recall. You perfectly remember the name and face of every person you’ve ever met.
    • Marked for Collection. You can use your action to mark a creature you can see within 120 feet of you for 1 hour. You have advantage on any Wisdom (Perception) or Wisdom (Survival) checks you make to find the marked creature. Additionally, as long as the creature is marked, you can discern whether or not the creature has passed through a location. You can have only one creature marked at a time.
    • Wanted Alive. Whenever you reduce a target to 0 hit points with any weapon attack you make, you can choose to knock the creature out instead of killing it.

    Chef

    Time spent mastering the culinary arts has paid off, granting you the following benefits:

    • Kitchen Savant. You gain proficiency with cook’s utensils if you don’t already have it.
    • Restorative Feast. As part of a short rest, you can cook special food, provided you have ingredients and cook’s utensils on hand. You can prepare enough of this food for a number of creatures equal to 4 + your Proficiency Bonus. At the end of the short rest, any creature who eats the food and spends one or more Hit Dice to regain hit points regains an extra 1d8 hit points.
    • Snacksmith. With one hour of work or when you finish a long rest, you can cook a number of treats equal to your Proficiency Bonus. These special treats last 8 hours after being made. A creature can use a bonus action to eat one of those treats to gain temporary hit points equal to your Proficiency Bonus.

    Combat Intuition

    You have an uncanny clarity of mind, allowing you to feel the ebb and flow of battle. You gain the following benefits:

    • Instinctive Striker. You have advantage on all opportunity attacks.
    • Heightened Awareness. You have advantage on all Wisdom (Perception) checks.

    Crossbow Expert

    Thanks to extensive practice with the crossbow, you gain the following benefits:

    • Rapid Reload. You ignore the Loading property of ranged weapons with which you are proficient.
    • Close-Quarters Sharpshooter. Being within 5 feet of a hostile creature doesn’t impose disadvantage on your ranged attack rolls.
    • Off-Hand Volley. When you use the Attack action and attack with a one-handed weapon, you can use a bonus action to attack with a hand crossbow you are holding.

    Defensive Duelist

    You’ve mastered fighting with only one weapon in your hands, using the free one to balance and help yourself in combat. You gain the following benefits:

    • Balanced Guard. When you are wielding one weapon in your hand and nothing in the other, you gain a +1 bonus to your Armor Class.
    • Reactive Riposte. When another creature that you can see hits you with a weapon attack while you are wielding only a Finesse or Versatile weapon with one hand, you can use your reaction to add your Proficiency Bonus to your Armor Class (potentially causing the attack to miss). If the attack misses because of this increase to your Armor Class, you can make an opportunity attack against the attacker as part of the same reaction if the attacker is within range.

    Diehard

    You use every last ounce of energy to survive, even in the worst of circumstances. You gain the following benefits:

    • Last Ditch Escape. When you are reduced to 0 or fewer hit points, you can use your reaction to move up to your speed before falling unconscious. Moving in this way doesn’t provoke opportunity attacks.
    • Fighting Chance. On the first turn that you start with 0 hit points and must make a death saving throw, you make that saving throw with advantage.
    • Reckless Resolve. When you take massive damage that would kill you instantly, you can use your reaction to make a death saving throw. If the saving throw succeeds, you instead fall unconscious and are dying.
    • Tough as Nails. Wisdom (Medicine) checks made to stabilize you have advantage.
    • Swift Recovery. When a creature successfully stabilizes you, at the start of your next turn you regain 1 hit point. Once you have used this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

    Dual Wielder

    You master fighting with two weapons, gaining the following benefits:

    • Unrestricted Dual Wielding. You can use two-weapon fighting even when the one-handed melee weapons you are wielding aren’t light.
    • Quick Draw. You can draw or stow two one-handed weapons when you would normally be able to draw or stow only one.
    • Dual Defense. You gain a +1 bonus to AC while you are wielding a separate melee weapon in each hand.
    • Effortless Combat. Whenever you use the Attack action, you don’t need to spend your bonus action to engage in two-weapon fighting.
    • Dual Counter. When you make an opportunity attack while wielding two one-handed melee weapons, you can also engage in two-weapon fighting, making only one attack with the weapon you’re holding in your other hand.

    Dungeon Delver

    You have an uncanny ability to deduce meaning from fragmented parts of a whole. You know when to duck to avoid traps and how to spot them in the first place. You gain the following benefits:

    • Trap Sense. You have advantage on ability checks and saving throws to detect, disarm, bypass, and avoid traps, locks, secret doors, and hidden compartments.
    • Darkvision. You gain darkvision out to a range of 60 feet.
    • Trap Resilience. You have resistance to the damage dealt by traps.
    • Unseen Awareness. You can notice creatures feigning death or attempting to pass themselves off as objects, such as those with the False Appearance trait, as long as your Passive Perception beats their Passive Stealth.
    • Script Decipher. You have advantage on ability checks to decipher faded or fragmented script.

    Eldritch Adept

    Prerequisites: Spellcasting or Pact Magic feature

    Studying occult lore, you learn one Eldritch Invocation option of your choice from the Warlock class. Your spellcasting ability for the invocation is Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma (choose when you select this feat). If the invocation has a prerequisite of any kind, you can choose that invocation only if you’re a Warlock who meets the prerequisite.

    Whenever you gain a level, you can replace the invocation with another one from the Warlock class.

    Elemental Adept

    Prerequisites: the ability to cast at least one spell

    In your spellcasting, you can harness a particular form of energy with deadly mastery. When you gain this feat, choose one of the following damage types: acid, cold, fire, lightning, or thunder. You then gain the following benefits:

    • Potent Channeling. When you roll damage for a spell you cast that deals damage of the chosen type, you can treat any 1 on a damage die as a 2.
    • Piercing Power. When you deal damage for a spell you cast that deals damage of the chosen type, you can choose to ignore resistance to that damage type on any targets of your choice.
    • Elemental Precision. When you deal damage for a spell you cast that deals damage of the chosen type, you can choose to apply resistance to that damage type to any targets of your choice.

    Ferocious Combatant

    You have trained to fight in a ferocious way in battle, gaining the following benefits:

    • Momentum Rush. When you take the Dash action, your speed increases by 10 feet for that action.
    • Relentless Impact. If you move at least 10 feet in a straight line immediately before hitting with a thrown weapon or melee attack as part of the Attack action on your turn, choose one of the following effects. You can only use this benefit once on each of your turns:
    • Deal extra damage equal to your proficiency bonus.
    • Push the target up to 10 feet, provided the target is no more than one size larger than you.
    • Savage Mark. When you score a critical hit with a melee weapon attack, the next attack roll made against the target before the end of your next turn has advantage. You can only use this benefit once on each of your turns.

    Firearm Expert

    Thanks to extensive practice with firearms, you gain the following benefits:

    • Point-Blank Precision. Being within 5 feet of a hostile creature doesn’t impose disadvantage on your ranged attack rolls.
    • Off-Hand Shot. When you use the Attack action and attack with a one-handed weapon, you can use a bonus action to attack with a loaded firearm with the Light property that you are holding.

    Grappler

    You’ve developed the skills necessary to hold your own in close-quarters grappling. You gain the following benefits:

    • Sure Grip. When you grapple a creature that doesn’t have this feat, your grapple attempt always succeeds if the result of your roll is at least the target’s Armor Class. You must still meet the other requirements for grappling.
    • Vicious Control. You have advantage on attack rolls against a creature you are grappling.
    • Pin and Lock. You can use your action to try to pin a creature grappled by you. To do so, make another grapple check. If you succeed, you and the creature are both restrained until the grapple ends.
    • Effortless Drag. You don’t spend extra movement when you move a creature grappled by you, provided the creature is your size or smaller.

    Great Weapon Master

    You’ve learned to use two-handed weapons to your advantage, letting their momentum empower your strikes. You gain the following benefits:

    • Powerful Swing. You add 1-1/2 times your Strength modifier (rounded up) to the damage rolls of melee weapons that you are wielding in two hands and that you are proficient with.
    • Reckless Momentum. Before you make a melee weapon attack with a melee weapon that you are wielding in two hands and that you are proficient with, you can take a penalty to the attack roll equal to your Proficiency Bonus. On a hit, you deal additional damage equal to double your Proficiency Bonus. This damage is of the same type as your weapon.
    • Unstoppable Cleave. On your turn, when you score a critical hit with a melee weapon that you are wielding in two hands and that you are proficient with, or reduce a creature to 0 hit points with a melee weapon attack, you gain an additional attack as part of your current Attack action until the end of your current turn. The maximum number of additional attacks you can gain this way is equal to your Proficiency Bonus.

    Healer

    You are an able physician, allowing you to mend wounds quickly and get your allies back in the fight. You gain the following benefits:

    • Trained Healer. You gain proficiency in the Medicine skill. Additionally, you have advantage on Wisdom (Medicine) checks to stabilize a creature without a healer’s kit.
    • Stabilizing Surge. When you use a healer’s kit to stabilize a dying creature, that creature also regains 1 hit point plus temporary hit points equal to your Proficiency Bonus.
    • Rapid Response. When a friendly creature receives a critical hit or is reduced to 0 hit points, you can spend your reaction to move up to your speed toward it. This movement does not provoke attacks of opportunity.
    • Restorative Treatment. As an action, you can spend one use of a healer’s kit to tend to a creature. The creature regains 1d6 + 4 hit points, plus additional hit points equal to its maximum number of hit dice. A creature can’t regain hit points from this feature again until it finishes a short or long rest.

    Inspiring Leader

    At the end of a short rest or a long rest, you can give an inspiring performance: a speech, a song, or a dance. When you do so, choose up to six friendly creatures (which can include yourself) within 30 feet of you who witness the performance. The chosen creatures each gain temporary hit points equal to your level + your Charisma modifier.

    Keen Mind

    You have an uncanny ability that can track time, direction, and detail with precision. You gain the following benefits:

    • Keen Investigator. You gain proficiency in the Investigation skill.
    • Natural Navigator. You always know which way is north and how many hours remain before the next sunrise or sunset.
    • Perfect Recall. You can accurately recall anything you have seen or heard within the past week.
    • Insightful Observer. You have advantage when you use the Study a Creature action.
    • Uncover the Unseen. When you meet a creature for the first time, you can automatically identify a subtle or unusual detail about them that reveals something not commonly known. For example, you might notice a limping duelist or detect a regional accent in a merchant’s speech.

    Linguist

    You have studied languages and codes, gaining the following benefits:

    • Cipher Crafter. You can ably create written ciphers. Others can’t decipher a code you create unless you teach them, they succeed on an Intelligence check (DC equal to your Intelligence score + your Proficiency Bonus), or they use magic to decipher it.
    • Linguistic Savant. You learn a number of languages equal to your Proficiency Bonus. Every time your Proficiency Bonus increases, you learn an additional language.

    Lucky

    You have inexplicable luck that can kick in at just the right moment, granting you the following benefits:

    • Luck Points. You have a number of Luck Points equal to your Proficiency Bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.
    • Twist of Fate. Whenever you make an attack roll, an ability check, or a saving throw, you can spend one Luck Point to grant yourself advantage on the roll. You can choose to spend the point after you roll the die, but before the outcome is determined.
    • Unlucky for Them. You can spend one Luck Point when an attack roll is made against you, causing the attacker to roll with disadvantage.

    Mage Slayer

    Your knowledge of magic and advanced tactical training gives you an edge when facing foes capable of spellcasting. You gain the following benefits:

    • Spellbreaker’s Strike. When a creature within 5 feet of you casts a spell, you can use your reaction to make a melee weapon attack against that creature. Alternatively, if you have a weapon with the Thrown property, you may draw and throw it as a reaction at a creature casting a spell within 20 feet.
    • Break Their Focus. When you damage a creature concentrating on a spell, it has disadvantage on the saving throw to maintain concentration.
    • Mental Fortress. If you fail an Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma saving throw, you can choose to succeed instead. Once you use this benefit, you can’t use it again until you finish a short or long rest.

    Magic Collector

    You possess an intuitive understanding of the way magic ebbs and flows within enchanted items. Such items attune easily to you, and you are able to sound out their secrets. You gain the following benefits:

    • Bound by Magic. You can attune to up to six magic items at once.
    • Mystic Insight. You can cast the identify and detect magic spells without expending a spell slot or material components. You must finish a long rest before you can do so again.
    • Wield What Others Cannot. You can ignore one restriction of a magic item related to class, race, or alignment.

    Magic Initiate

    You have learned the basics of a particular magical tradition. Choose a spellcasting class related to a certain theme: Arcane (Bard, Sorcerer, Warlock, Wizard), Divine (Cleric, Paladin), or Primal (Druid, Ranger). You gain the following benefits related to that choice:

    • Theme Proficiency. You gain proficiency in the Arcana, Religion, or Nature skill, depending on the theme of the class you chose.
    • Spellcasting Cantrips. You learn two cantrips of your choice from the chosen class spell list.
    • Magical Knowledge. You learn one 1st-level spell from the chosen class spell list. You always have the spell prepared, and it doesn’t count against your number of Spells Known nor your number of Prepared Spells. You can cast it once without spending a spell slot, and you regain the ability to cast it in that way when you finish a long rest. You can also cast the spell using any spell slots you have.
    • Spellcasting Ability. Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma is your spellcasting ability for these spells (choose when you select this feat).
    • Flexible Spell Choices. Whenever you gain a new level, you can replace one of the spells you chose for this feat with a different spell of the same level from the chosen spell list.
    • Repeatable. You can take this feat more than once, but you must choose a different class each time.

    Martial Adept

    Your martial training allows you to perform special combat maneuvers and has helped you develop a particular style of fighting. You gain the following benefits:

    • Fighting Style. You learn one Fighting Style option of your choice from the Fighter Class. If you already have a style, the one you choose must be different.
    • Martial Exploits. You learn two Martial Exploits of your choice from among those available to the Fighter Class. If a maneuver you use requires your target to make a saving throw to resist the maneuver’s effects, the saving throw DC equals 8 + your Proficiency Bonus + your Strength or Dexterity modifier (your choice).
    • Exploit Dice. You gain two Exploit Dice, which are a d6 (these dice are added to any exploit dice you have from another source). These dice are used to fuel your exploits. An exploit die is expended when you use it. You regain your expended exploit dice when you finish a short rest or long rest.

    Master Poisoner

    Your expertise in toxicology grants you the following benefits:

    • Toxic Savant. You gain proficiency with the poisoner’s kit and have advantage on ability checks made using it.
    • Through the Veins. When you make a damage roll, you ignore resistance to poison damage.
    • Sense the Stench. As an action, you can automatically detect the presence of any nonmagical poison within 5 feet that you can see or smell.
    • Refined Concoction. You can dedicate 1 hour to improving an existing poison. You must use a poisoner’s kit and spend 10% of the poison’s initial value in rare ingredients. The poison’s save DC increases by your Proficiency Bonus. A poison can be improved only once.
    • Iron Stomach. When you fail a saving throw against a poison, you can use your reaction to reroll it. The new result replaces the original.

    Metamagic Adept

    You’ve learned how to exert your will on your spells to alter how they function. You gain the following benefits:

    • Metamagic Options. You learn two Metamagic options of your choice from the Sorcerer Class. You can use only one Metamagic option on a spell when you cast it, unless the option says otherwise. Whenever you gain a new level, you can replace one of these Metamagic options with another one from the Sorcerer Class.
    • Sorcery Points. You gain 2 Sorcery Points to spend on Metamagic (these points are added to any Sorcery Points you have from another source but can be used only on Metamagic). You regain all spent Sorcery Points when you finish a long rest.

    Mobile

    You are exceptionally speedy and agile. You gain the following benefits:

    • Fleet of Foot. Your speed increases by 10 feet.
    • Unimpeded Dash. When you use the Dash action, difficult terrain doesn’t cost you extra movement on that turn.
    • Dancing Strike. When you make a melee attack against a creature, you don’t provoke opportunity attacks from that creature for the rest of the turn, whether you hit or not.

    Mounted Combatant

    You were raised riding a horse or a similar animal, and were always eager to mount up and ride off. You gain the following benefits:

    • Animal Whisperer. You gain proficiency in Animal Handling.
    • Mounted Bond. You gain advantage on your Animal Handling checks made to handle or train horses and other beasts employed as mounts.
    • Unstoppable Charge. While mounted, you gain advantage on all melee attack rolls against unmounted creatures which are smaller than your mount.
    • Shield of the Steed. When your mount is attacked with a melee or ranged weapon attack, as a reaction you can redirect the triggering attack to yourself.
    • Steady Hoof. When your mount is required to make a Dexterity saving throw against a spell or effect that deals half damage with a successful save, as a reaction you may roll an Animal Handling check instead of your mount’s Dexterity saving throw. If your check is successful, your mount suffers no damage from the triggering effect, and half damage if it fails.

    Observant

    Quick to notice details of your environment, you gain the following benefits:

    • Silent Speech. If you can see a creature’s mouth while it is speaking a language you understand, you can interpret what it’s saying by reading its lips.
    • Nothing Escapes Me. You have a +5 bonus to your passive Wisdom (Perception) and passive Intelligence (Investigation) scores.

    Polearm Master

    You can keep your enemies at bay with reach weapons. You gain the following benefits:

    • Sweeping Follow-Through. When you take the Attack action and attack with only a Reach weapon, a quarterstaff, or a spear, you can use a bonus action to make a melee attack with the opposite end of the weapon. This attack uses the same ability modifier as the primary attack, deals 1d4 bludgeoning damage, and adds your ability modifier.
    • Hobbling Strike. Once per turn when you hit a creature with a Reach weapon, a quarterstaff, or a spear, you can reduce the target’s speed by 10 feet until the start of your next turn.
    • Zone of Control. While you are wielding a Reach weapon, a quarterstaff, or a spear, other creatures provoke an opportunity attack from you when they enter your reach.
    • Pierce the Gap. Attacks you make with Reach weapons, a quarterstaff, or a spear ignore cover provided by creatures adjacent to you.

    Resilient

    You have taken the time to fortify either your mind or your body. Choose one ability score.

    • Unshakable Foundation. If you are not already proficient in saving throws using the chosen ability score, you gain proficiency in them.
    • Extra Resilience. If you are already proficient in saving throws using the chosen ability score, you can use your reaction to gain advantage on a saving throw using that ability. You can use this feature three times, and regain all expended uses after finishing a long rest.

    Resolute Caster

    Your magic clings to the world even when your mind falters. When you lose concentration on a spell—whether due to taking damage, falling unconscious, or even dying—the spell continues until the start of your next turn. If you die, the spell ends at the point when your next turn would have begun, had you survived.

    Ritual Caster

    You have delved into the patient art of ritual magic, recording spells for use in precise, deliberate castings.

    • Ritual Book. You gain a ritual book containing two 1st-level spells of your choice from one of the following classes: Bard, Cleric, Druid, Sorcerer, Warlock, or Wizard. The spells must have the Ritual tag. You must have the ritual book in hand to cast spells from it.
    • Spellcasting Ability. Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma is your spellcasting ability for these spells (choose when you select this feat).
    • Spell Scribe. When you find a spell in written form (e.g., a spell scroll or Wizard’s spellbook), you can attempt to add it to your ritual book. The spell must be on the spell list of your chosen class, have the Ritual tag and cannot be of a level no higher than half your character level (rounded up). Copying a spell into your ritual book takes 2 hours per spell level and costs 50 gp per level in special inks and materials.

    Sage’s Insight

    Your reputation precedes you, and you are known as an expert in your chosen fields of study. Fellow scholars treat you with respect and may even seek you out for advice.

    • Academic Eminence. Choose three fields of study from the following table. You may only choose a field if you are proficient with its associated skill.

    Fields of Study

    Field of Study Skill
    Ancient mysteries, magic traditions, arcane symbols, constructs, dragons, magical beasts Arcana
    Legends, personalities, inhabitants, laws, customs, traditions, humanoids Culture
    Buildings, aqueducts, bridges, fortifications Engineering
    Wars, colonies, migrations, founding of cities, myths and legends History
    Animals, fey, monstrous humanoids, plants, seasons and cycles, weather, vermin Nature
    Gods and goddesses, mythic history, ecclesiastic tradition, holy symbols, undead Religion
    • Subject Matter Expert. You gain advantage on all Intelligence checks related to one of your chosen fields of study.
    • Reputation of Insight. Your DM may rule that certain Intelligence checks require an appropriate field of study to attempt or may significantly lower the DC of the check if you have an applicable field.
    • Custom Curriculum. Alternatively, you may create a new field of study (with your DM’s permission) tied to a skill in which you are proficient.

    Sentinel

    You have mastered techniques to take advantage of every drop in an enemy’s guard, gaining the following benefits:

    • Arresting Blow. When you hit a creature with an opportunity attack, that creature’s speed becomes 0 for the rest of the turn.
    • Punish the Overreach. Immediately after a creature within 5 feet of you takes the Disengage action or hits a target other than you with an attack, you can make an opportunity attack against that creature.

    Sharpshooter

    You have spent countless hours training with ranged weapons, learning how to place the arrow or bullet exactly where you want it. You gain the following benefits when attacking with a ranged weapon:

    • Extended Precision. Attacking at long range doesn’t impose disadvantage on your ranged weapon attack rolls.
    • Cover is No Obstacle. When making ranged weapon attacks, you treat three-quarters cover as half cover, and half cover as no cover.
    • Risk for Reward. Before you make a ranged weapon attack using a weapon that you are proficient with, you can take a penalty to the attack roll equal to your Proficiency Bonus. On a hit, you deal additional damage equal to double your Proficiency Bonus. This damage is of the same type as your weapon.

    Shield Bearer

    You are a fearsome foe with a shield, capable of skillfully protecting yourself and striking back at your enemies. As long as you are equipped with a shield, you gain the following benefits:

    • Immovable Defense. If you do not move during your turn, you gain half cover until the start of your next turn. This benefit does not apply if you are incapacitated.
    • Reflexive Riposte. Whenever a creature misses you with a melee attack, you can use your reaction to automatically deal your attacker bludgeoning damage equal to 1d6 + your Strength modifier.

    Shield Master

    You use shields not just for protection but also for offense. You gain the following benefits while wielding a shield:

    • Shield Slam. When you hit a creature within 5 feet of you with a melee weapon attack as part of the Attack action, you can bash the target with your shield. The target must succeed on a Strength saving throw (DC = 8 + your Proficiency Bonus + your Strength modifier) or be knocked prone or pushed 5 feet away. You can use this benefit once per turn.
    • Reflexive Guard. If you aren’t incapacitated, you can add your shield’s AC bonus to any Dexterity saving throw you make against a spell or harmful effect that targets only you.
    • Interposing Defense. When subjected to an effect that allows a Dexterity saving throw to take half damage, you can use your reaction to take no damage on a successful save, interposing your shield between yourself and the effect.

    Skulker

    You are expert at slinking through shadows. You gain the following benefits:

    • Kindled Shadows. You learn the control flames cantrip, and you can cast it as a bonus action.
    • Eyes in the Gloom. You gain blindsight with a range of 10 feet. Additionally, dim light doesn’t impose disadvantage on your Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on sight.
    • Combat Slipstream. You have advantage on any Dexterity (Stealth) check you make as part of the Hide action during combat.
    • Silent Miss. If you make an attack roll while hidden and the roll misses, you remain hidden—making the attack does not reveal your position.
    • Dark Veil. Creatures cannot use blindsight to detect you when you attempt to hide.

    Smooth Grappler

    Your knowledge of leverage and pressure points enables you to take advantage of your opponents’s weight and momentum to efficiently wrestle them and throw them down. You gain the following benefits:

    • Graceful Grappler. You can use Dexterity (Athletics) instead of Strength (Athletics) when grappling or shoving a creature.
    • Strike and Seize. Once per turn, when you hit a creature with an unarmed strike, you can immediately use your reaction to attempt to grapple or shove that target.

    Spell Mastery

    You have mastered one spell above all others and are adept at using it with unmatched precision and power. Choose one spell of 1st-level or higher that you can cast using your spell slots. This spell must be from a spellcasting class in which you have levels. You can change this spell when you gain a new level in that class. You gain the following benefits:

    • Imprinted Knowledge. The chosen spell no longer counts against your number of known or prepared spells, allowing you to learn or prepare an additional spell from your class’s list.
    • Empowered Casting. When you cast your signature spell, you do so as if using a spell slot one level higher than the slot actually expended (to a maximum of 6th level).
    • Unbreakable Focus. When a creature attempts to counter or dispel your signature spell with counterspell or dispel magic, they must succeed on an ability check using their spellcasting ability, regardless of the level at which your spell was cast.

    Spell Sniper

    You have learned techniques to enhance your attacks with certain kinds of spells, gaining the following benefits:

    • Extended Reach. When you cast a spell that requires you to make an attack roll, the spell’s range is doubled.
    • Piercing Precision. When making ranged spell attacks, you treat three-quarters cover as half cover, and half cover as no cover.
    • Arcane Sharpshooter. You learn one cantrip that requires an attack roll. Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma is your spellcasting ability for this cantrip (choose when you select this feat).

    Swift

    Your astounding alacrity and fluid movements grant you the following benefits:

    • Fleet of Foot. Your speed increases by 5 feet.
    • Quick Quaff. You may drink the contents of a vial (such as a potion) as a bonus action instead of an action. This benefit can be used only once per round, and only on your turn.
    • Unhindered Reload. You ignore the loading property of weapons.
    • Swift Recovery. When you are prone, standing up uses only 5 feet of your movement.

    Tavern Brawler

    Accustomed to rough-and-tumble fighting using whatever weapons happen to be at hand, you gain the following benefits:

    • Heavy Hands. When you hit with your unarmed strike and deal damage, you can deal bludgeoning damage equal to 1d4 + your Strength modifier instead of the normal unarmed strike damage.
    • Street Tough. Whenever you roll a damage die for your unarmed strike or an improvised weapon, you can reroll the die if it rolls a 1. You must use the new roll.
    • Momentum Slam. When you hit a creature with an unarmed strike or an improvised weapon as part of the Attack action on your turn, you can deal damage and also push the creature 5 feet or attempt to grapple it. You can use this benefit only once per turn.
    • Anything Is a Weapon. You are proficient with improvised weapons.
    • Furniture Fighter. You can wield furniture as weapons: small or medium furniture uses the stats of a greatclub, while tiny furniture uses the stats of a club.

    Thrower

    Your ease and accuracy with thrown weapons is such that you gain the following benefits when you make a ranged attack using a weapon with the thrown property:

    • Quick Toss. As a bonus action, you can make another ranged attack using a weapon with the Thrown property.
    • Piercing Accuracy. When making ranged weapon attacks with a weapon with the Thrown property, you treat 3/4 cover as 1/2 cover, and 1/2 cover as no cover.
    • Unfazed by Proximity. Being within 5 feet of a hostile creature doesn’t impose disadvantage on your ranged attack rolls with weapons that have the Thrown property.

    Toughness

    Hardy and resilient, you gain the following benefits:

    • Tough as Nails. Your hit point maximum is increased by +1 per hit die, and you increase your hit point maximum by +1 whenever you gain a hit die (such as when you gain a new level).
    • Renewed Vigor. Any time you spend a hit die to recover hit points during a short or long rest, you recover an additional 2 hit points per hit die used to recover hit points.

    Two-Weapon Mastery

    You master fighting with two weapons, gaining the following benefits:

    • Versatile Fighter. You can use two-weapon fighting even when the one-handed melee weapons you are wielding aren’t Light.
    • Swift Draw. You can draw or stow two one-handed weapons when you would normally be able to draw or stow only one.
    • Shielding Strikes. You gain a +1 bonus to Armor Class while you are wielding a separate melee weapon in each hand.
    • Seamless Assault. Whenever you use the Attack action, you don’t need to spend your bonus action to engage in two-weapon fighting.
    • Quick Counter. When you make an opportunity attack while wielding two one-handed melee weapons, you can also engage in two-weapon fighting, making only one attack with the weapon you’re holding in the other hand.

    Unarmored Defense

    You’ve mastered the art of fighting without armor because of circumstances of your environment or life. You gain the following benefits:

    • Shield Proficiency. You become proficient with shields.
    • Unarmored Defense. You gain the Unarmored Defense feature as long as you don’t wear armor. Your Armor Class equals 10 + your Dexterity modifier + your Constitution modifier. You can use a shield and still gain this benefit.

    War Caster

    You have practiced casting spells in the midst of combat, learning techniques that grant you the following benefits:

    • Concentration Mastery. You have advantage on Constitution saving throws that you make to maintain your concentration on a spell when you take damage.
    • Weapon-Somatic Flexibility. You can perform the somatic components of spells even when you have weapons or a shield in one or both hands.
    • Spellcasting Opportunity. When a hostile creature’s movement provokes an opportunity attack from you, you can use your reaction to cast a spell at the creature, rather than making an opportunity attack. The spell must have a casting time of 1 action and must target only that creature.

    Well-Traveled

    You have been traveling the world for years and know your way around. Choose a number of cities equal to your Proficiency Bonus as your list of well-known cities. Whenever your Proficiency Bonus increases, you add another city to the list. You have advantage on all Intelligence (History) rolls when it pertains to one of your chosen cities. While you are in a city that is on your list of well-known cities, you gain the following benefits:

    • Urban Tracker. You may use Charisma (Persuasion) or Intelligence (Investigation) instead of Wisdom (Survival) to track someone.
    • Hidden in Plain Sight. You may conceal your presence in the city. If you wish to keep your presence secret, anyone asking for you or attempting to determine where you are through nonmagical means must succeed on an Investigation check (DC = 8 + your Proficiency Bonus + your Intelligence or Charisma modifier).
    • Local Bargain Hunter. You get a 10% discount on all non-magical equipment.
    • City Rumormonger. You are always aware of the most popular rumors in the city.
    • Never Lost. You cannot get lost and you know the streets quite well. You know what areas to avoid and which areas are safer than others. You know the quickest way between two points in the city.
    • Innkeeper’s Knowledge. You always know where the inns and taverns are. If you set your mind to it, you can always find a room, even during major events. Knowing the right people helps.
    • City Connections. You have made a few solid connections. The nature of these connections is up to you. They may be lesser well-connected nobles, powerful merchants, or someone with pull in the city’s underworld. You may use your connections to gain information or to seek an audience with such individuals, though such a meeting may entail a cost, one not always paid in coin.
